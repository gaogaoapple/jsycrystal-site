7 Gemstones to Start a Crystal Bracelet Collection for Your Brand

A first collection does not need twenty stones. It needs a few colors that photograph clearly, sit well next to each other, and can be mixed in one order. At JSY Crystal every order is 100 pieces mixed per order. Quotes are EXW from our factory in Ketang, Shanwei.

The seven stones below are common starter picks for that reason. 8 mm round beads are a useful default size. Smaller beads look finer. Larger beads read bolder. You can mix sizes and stones inside the 100 pieces. Color varies from bead to bead because the stone is natural. We confirm the look with you before production.

1. Citrine

Citrine runs from pale yellow to a deeper gold. It is an easy warm color for a shop photo and it does not fight with darker beads. Pair it with tiger eye when you want two warm tones, or with amethyst when you want a clear contrast. An 8 mm round is a common size for a first citrine bracelet. If you already sell yellow jewelry, start here rather than with a stone you have not tested. See the citrine bracelet page for the bead we show.

2. Rose quartz

Rose quartz is a soft pink. It is one of the stones people recognize without a label. It pairs with amethyst, with clear quartz, and with a small metal spacer if your design uses one. Pale and deeper pink beads can sit in the same order, as long as you approve the range from a sample or a photo. For a gift-leaning brand, rose quartz is often the largest share of the first 100 pieces. The rose quartz bracelet page shows the style.

3. Amethyst

Amethyst covers a wide purple, from light lilac to a dark violet. A pale bracelet and a deep bracelet can be two styles in the same mixed order. It sits next to rose quartz in a stack, and next to citrine when you want purple and gold in one photo. 8 mm rounds are the usual starting size. Agree the color range before stringing. The amethyst bracelet page is the reference we publish.

4. Tiger eye

Tiger eye is gold-brown with a band of light that moves as the bead turns. That flash holds up in a product photo. Yellow, golden, red and blue tiger eye are different looks. Do not treat them as one color. If tiger eye is the core of the brand, use the tiger eye wholesale page and say which styles belong in the 100 pieces. An 8 mm or 10 mm round is common.

5. Lapis lazuli

Lapis lazuli is a deep blue, often with lighter flecks. It does not look like amethyst or tiger eye, so it adds a color the other six do not cover. It pairs with rose quartz, and it also works as a single-stone style. Flecks vary. Say whether you want a more even blue or a more mottled bead. We do not publish a separate lapis page yet. Ask for a current photo with the quote.

6. Green aventurine

Green aventurine is a green stone with a soft sparkle. It fills the gap if the rest of the collection is pink, purple and gold. It pairs with rose quartz and with citrine. 8 mm rounds mix with the other stones in this list. Ask for a current photo. The approved sample is the reference, because the green varies by batch.

7. Rainbow obsidian

Rainbow obsidian is a dark stone with bands or flashes of color. It is not the same bracelet as the golden obsidian or silver obsidian already shown on this site. Those are separate looks. Use rainbow obsidian when you want a dark bracelet that still has color when it turns. It pairs with tiger eye and with a single lighter bead if the design needs a break. Keep the first quantity modest inside the 100 pieces until you have seen the flash in a photo of the actual beads. Ask for that photo.

A seven-color mix

You can also put several of these colors on one bracelet. Treat that as an extra style, not a replacement for the single-stone styles. Name the colors you want. We will not add a meaning you did not write. 8 mm rounds keep the colors readable. If one color should dominate, say so.

How to split a 100-piece mixed first order

One hundred pieces is enough for a small shop launch and small enough to correct after you see what sells. A plain split is 20 citrine, 20 rose quartz, 15 amethyst, 15 tiger eye, 10 lapis lazuli, 10 green aventurine and 10 rainbow obsidian. That is 100. Change the numbers to match your market. If your reference sites are mostly pink and purple, move pieces from the green and the dark stone into rose quartz and amethyst.

Keep most of the order at one bead size, usually 8 mm, and use a second size only on one or two styles. Mixing every stone in every size makes the first reorder harder to read. Finished stock bracelets, if you also want styles we already make, follow the same 100-piece mixed MOQ.

Write the split in the inquiry. “Not sure” is acceptable. We can suggest a starter mix from your market and from the sites you send. The quote covers the bracelets and any tags or packaging you add. It is EXW Ketang, Shanwei. The shipping method and the carrier are discussed for that order. We do not lock a carrier or a transit time on this page.

Tags, pouches, boxes and cards

Three items cover a first unboxing: a custom logo tag, a branded pouch or gift box, and a printed card. The card can carry a short brand story or the stone name. You write the words. We print what you approve. We do not add test reports, lab names, or care lines about water. Tags, pouches, boxes and cards are confirmed with the design, including their own minimums. Beads, tags and packaging are handled by one team. If a sample is part of the project, the sample fee can be credited against the bulk order. A design can be kept exclusive. An NDA is available. We quote the project. There is no public price.

What to send with the inquiry

Send three things. First, the market you sell into, in plain words: the country, the kind of shop, and who buys. Second, two or three reference websites, with a note on what you like and what you do not want copied. Third, the stone split, even if the numbers are rough. A photo of a bracelet helps.
