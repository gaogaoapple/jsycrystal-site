MOQ, Samples & Lead Times: A First-Time Buyer's Guide to Importing Crystal Bracelets from China

How MOQ, sample costs and production lead times really work when importing crystal bracelets from China — with the timelines, shipping options and budget lines first-time buyers usually miss.

First-time importers usually ask three questions in the wrong order: price first, MOQ second, timing last. Timing is actually the question that decides whether an order succeeds, because a bracelet program lives on launch dates, gift seasons and replenishment. This guide walks through the three numbers that matter — MOQ, samples and lead times — using the way our own orders run as the example.

MOQ: minimum order quantity, decoded

MOQ is the smallest quantity a supplier will produce or release for a given item. It can be set per style, per bead size, or per total order — always ask which one you are being quoted. Low headline MOQs with high per-size minimums are a common trap.

  • Standard orders: at JSY Crystal the minimum is 10 pieces per order in total — and because mixing across stone types and sizes is free, those 10 pieces can be ten different styles if you want to test broadly.
  • Custom and private label work: quantities are confirmed per project, because cards, tags and packaging have their own minimums set by the printers and workshops involved.
  • Mixed orders: small-batch mixed orders across stone types and sizes are supported — the practical way to build a first range without over-buying any single style.

Samples: what they cost and why they are not optional

A sample is your only chance to judge color grading, bead finish and stringing quality before bulk money moves. Skipping samples to save a week is the most expensive shortcut in this category.

  • Cost: samples are charged at cost with us; the buyer pays shipping.
  • Deduction: the sample fee can be deducted from a first order that reaches the qualifying quantity — ask for the threshold in writing.
  • Timing: sampling time depends on whether the design is a standard style or a new development; it is confirmed per project before you commit.
  • What to check: bead color against the listing photos, size accuracy with a caliper, elastic tension, and how the bracelet looks in daylight — most returns in this category are color surprises, not breakage.

Lead times: production and shipping are different clocks

Quotes often blend production and delivery into one number. Separate them:

StageTypical timingNotes
Inquiry & quotationWithin 24 hoursFaster when you send stone, size, quantity and destination country up front.
SamplingConfirmed per projectStandard styles are quicker than new custom developments.
Bulk production15–30 daysDepends on quantity, customization and packaging.
Express shipping (DHL / FedEx)About 7–10 daysBest for launches, samples and replenishment.
Sea freightAbout 25–35 daysLowest cost per piece for larger, planned orders.

Work backwards from your selling date: bulk production (up to 30 days) + shipping (up to 10 days express, 35 by sea) + a buffer for inspection and customs. A gift-season launch planned in October should be in production by early September at the latest on sea freight.

The budget lines first-timers forget

  • Sample fees and sample shipping (partly recoverable — see the deduction above).
  • Customization: printed cards, gift boxes, pouches and tags are quoted per project, not folded into the bracelet price automatically.
  • Freight and insurance, which swing with season and destination.
  • Import duties and taxes in your country — your customs broker, not your supplier, owns this number.
  • A small contingency for replenishment: the order after the successful first one is the one that usually runs late.

Putting it together

Send your supplier five things in the first message — stone types, bead sizes, estimated quantity per style, customization needs, and destination country with your target date — and you will get a usable quotation for your brief.
